**Section 1: Why This Matters to Your Business**

A short 2–3 paragraph section that contextualizes the ESOP topic for the reader's specific situation. Written in plain English, not jargon. The goal is to make them feel understood, not lectured.

*Example for Compliance & Governance Guide:*
> You have already taken the important step of setting up an ESOP for your team. That is a meaningful commitment to your employees and a signal that you are building a company people want to stay in for the long term.
>
> However, an ESOP without a formal, independent valuation is a plan with a missing foundation. The valuation is not just a number. It is the document that anchors your option pricing, protects you in an audit, and gives your employees a credible basis for understanding what their equity is worth.
>
> In Singapore, the absence of a formal valuation can create significant exposure — particularly during fundraising, audit, or when an employee exercises their options and asks how the strike price was determined.

---

**Section 2: What Good Looks Like**

A brief explanation of what a properly structured ESOP valuation process looks like, and why it matters for retention, compliance, and investor confidence.

One critical point to address here: **an ESOP is only as valuable as the employee's ability to eventually benefit from it.** Many companies set up an option pool without thinking through the liquidity event. If there is no secondary market or planned exit, employees may hold options for years without ever being able to cash out. A well-structured ESOP plan should address this from the start — including how and when employees can exercise options and whether secondary transactions are possible. Bain Squared can advise on structuring the plan with liquidity in mind, not just the valuation number.

Another common gap: **most cap table management software providers are not advisors.** They provide the system for HR to manage grants and track the cap table. They do not advise on valuation methodology, liaise with auditors, or help communicate the option value to employees. This leaves founders and HR teams to navigate the valuation process alone — which is exactly where things go wrong.
